# The assistants, the exploring loop and the knowledge loop: review register

**Open Science Pillars (OSP), a personal project (personal hat) · 2026-11-26 · what was attacked and read, by whom, what each found, what changed, what remains.**

**Said first, because it decides how to read the rest.** Five things are recorded here, and they are not the same kind of evidence.

1. **The builder's self-review of the tools.** The assistant that built this part attacked its own tools: eleven findings, eight fixed and three stated as limits. It is not independent.
2. **An independent review by a working hydrologist** (altimetry and gauges), who read the single-instance run of the Baton Rouge demo cold, recomputed it with their own code, tried the tools on a river of their own, and did not open the run with separate assistants. Fourteen findings. Each is listed below with what became of it.
3. **What separate assistants found.** Six assistants, each started from its role file, ran the demo again (`examples/baton-rouge/with-assistants`), and each ended its answer with what was unclear, wrong or in the way in the role files, the skills and the tools. Those are listed below with what became of each.
4. **An independent attack on the tools**, by a reviewer who did not build them, working in a private copy on 2026-11-26. Twelve findings: 2 blockers, 5 serious, 5 minor, each shown by running the tool. Each is listed in section 4 with what became of it.
5. **The fixes for that attack have been tested by their author only.** One author made all twelve fixes, wrote the self-test for each, and tried two or three variations of each attack that the reviewer had not written down. That is the same kind of evidence as round 1: it finds real holes and it is not independent. **A second independent attack is owed**, and until it is run, every fix in section 4 stands as "closed as far as its author could break it".

## What still stands

- **A second independent attack is owed.** The first is section 4. Its twelve fixes were made and tested by one author and have not been attacked by anyone else.
- **The kernel fence was built and tested on one Linux machine.** Nothing is known about it elsewhere. On a machine without it, the fence around a look is the scratch folder and the reading of what comes back, and the receipt says so.
- **Nothing built here keeps a person or an assistant with a shell from reading set-aside data outside the tool.** That is the study tool's first limit, and no fix in section 4 changes it.
- **The Claude Code runner has not been run against Claude Code.** Its scripts test themselves. The run by separate assistants held the roles by instruction and the role file, not through the runner.
- **No assistant has been started from `assistants/coordinating.md`.** The coordinating role was played by the person's session.
- **The hand-over has not been made by separate assistants.** The only hand-over is the single-instance run's.
- **One model was behind every role.** Separate contexts found what self-review missed; they do not make separate minds.
- **A script can still reach what its command does not name** over the network, in the system's folders and the launcher's cache, and, where there is no kernel fence, by a path it builds itself. A tool of this package called from inside the script is now refused.
- **Times kept as numbers, and data that is not text, are outside the fence's reading.** Data that is not text is refused unless it is a figure in `figures/`, which the fence cannot read either.
- **A site under a wholly different name** (another agency's identifier for the same gauge) is not matched.
- **What is read before data is set aside** is known to the tool only as the page it is given.
- **Nothing was run against the OpenWiki tool.**
- **A9, A10 and A11 of the builder's round**: a lead attached as a finding after a real opening unless `check-study` is run; a reviewer is a typed name, now one that must be in the log before the lead; subjects are matched by text.
- **The overlap check cannot read meaning**, and no tool checks that a source supports a statement.
