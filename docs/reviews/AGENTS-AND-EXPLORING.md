# The agents, the exploring loop and the knowledge loop: the detail behind the register

*History, kept as it was written on 2026-11-26, in the words of that day ("assistant" for what is now called an agent). Sections 1 to 4 name files by the paths of the day and cite records that are at the branch `records-2026-11` (docs/history/RENAMES.md); sections 5 to 7 use today's paths. The register, with a unique label for every finding and what is owed now, is `docs/reviews/README.md`.*


**Said first, because it decides how to read the rest.** Seven things are recorded here, and they are not the same kind of evidence.

1. **The builder's self-review of the tools.** The assistant that built this part attacked its own tools: eleven findings, eight fixed and three stated as limits. It is not independent.
2. **An independent review by a working hydrologist** (altimetry and gauges), who read the single-instance run of the Baton Rouge demo cold, recomputed it with their own code, tried the tools on a river of their own, and did not open the run with separate assistants. Fourteen findings. Each is listed below with what became of it.
3. **What separate assistants found.** Six assistants, each started from its role file, ran the demo again (`examples/baton-rouge/with-assistants`), and each ended its answer with what was unclear, wrong or in the way in the role files, the skills and the tools. Those are listed below with what became of each.
4. **An independent attack on the tools**, by a reviewer who did not build them, working in a private copy on 2026-11-26. Twelve findings: 2 blockers, 5 serious, 5 minor, each shown by running the tool. Each is listed in section 4 with what became of it.
5. **A second independent attack on the tools**, by another reviewer who built none of them and made none of the fixes, on 2026-11-26. Of the twelve earlier findings eight held as fixed, two held as written with the claim behind them broken by a new route, and two were partly closed; and there were fourteen new findings: 2 blockers, 4 serious, 8 minor. Each is listed in section 5 with what became of it.
6. **The fixes for both attacks have been tested by their author only.** One author made the twelve fixes of section 4 and the fourteen of section 5, wrote the self-test for each, and tried variations the reviewers had not written down. That is the same kind of evidence as round 1: it finds real holes and it is not independent. A third independent attack was owed, including the pull request route of section 6; it is item 7.
7. **A third independent attack on the tools**, by a reviewer who built none of them and made none of the fixes, on 2026-11-26, the pull request route included. Of the second attack's fourteen findings it found twelve closed or honestly restated, two living only in the harness layer and not re-broken, and N13 still open as the pages said. New: 1 serious and 3 minor, no blocker. The serious one: on a clone, with ordinary git and an approvals file written by hand, the whole pull request route was forged and recorded as a real act. Each is listed in section 7 with what became of it. **Those fixes are again tested by their author only, and a fourth independent attack is owed.**

## 6. The lock and the signature as pull requests

**What was built.** The two acts are declared in `factory/factory.yaml` (who, how many approvals, the protected branch, what a person is shown). `sf.py request-lock` and `request-signature` write a request into the study: an evidence summary in plain words and a manifest of checksums; they record nothing as done, and an assistant may run them. **What was tested, and how.** The route end to end in a throwaway git repository with a branch named `main` standing in for a protected branch: a request, the check on payloads written in the platform's shapes, a merge, the recording, the opening, and the same for the signature up to a study that closes as a signed finding. **What git alone cannot show**, and the documents now say: who approved, and that the branch is protected. A local branch with the protected branch's name looks the same from a clone. The platform's record is where that is checked.

**Who fixed and who tested.** One author made every fix below and wrote every self-test, on 2026-11-26, and reproduced A3-1 from the report's own commands against the tree as it stood (the forged lock was recorded as `route: pull-request` and the held-out data opened) and against the fixed tree. **These fixes are tested by their author only. The route has still never run on GitHub, and the verification has never asked GitHub anything.**
