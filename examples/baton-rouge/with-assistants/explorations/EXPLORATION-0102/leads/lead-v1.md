# Lead from EXPLORATION-0102 (exploring; version 1)

**Exploring.** This is a lead, not a finding. It was found by looking at data, every choice in it was made after looking, and it has been tested on nothing. It cannot be signed. To become a finding it goes through a confirming study on the data that was set aside.

**The question.** Does the SWOT RiverSP Version D reach width for reach 74210000331 rise and fall with the stage at USGS-07374000, well enough to serve as a check on the height?

**What looks true.** At reach 74210000331 (Mississippi at Baton Rouge), calendar 2024, collection SWOT_L2_HR_RiverSP_reach_D, the reach width looks as if it does rise and fall with the stage at USGS-07374000, but only when passes of one overpass track are read together. Inside a track the width grows by about 7 m per metre of stage (slopes +9.86, +7.09 and +6.50 m of width per m of stage on tracks A, B and C; n=17, 18 and 15 pairs; r=+0.762, +0.865 and +0.973), over a stage span of 1.47 to 10.33 m. Across tracks it does not hold as a usable relation: track A reports a wider river than track B in 17 of 17 pairs of passes one or two days apart (mean difference -39.3 m, with the stage moving at most 0.652 m inside a pair), so over all 50 pairs the correlation is +0.683 with a residual of 25.1 m, which is 3.23 m of stage, more than the year's stage standard deviation of 2.99 m. Changes between consecutive passes, the form the signed recipe uses for height, correlate at only +0.274 for width against +0.9567 for height, because consecutive passes alternate tracks. As a check on the height it looks coarse: the scatter of height about a per-track width line is 1.81 m (median, leave one out), so only gross height errors could be seen. With three track lines and a 3 scatter threshold chosen after looking, 3 of 52 passes are singled out: the two whose height departs from the gauge (2024-03-26 and 2024-10-30) and one whose height agrees with the gauge and whose width is the odd one (2024-02-12, 937.3 m). On 2024-03-26 the width itself is off (29.9 m below its track line, dark_frac 0.692), so there the check fires for the reason the scientist feared, a bad pass spoiling both quantities. The gauge is provisional throughout.

**How it was found.** Read the knowledge first. The signed pages give the pairing rule, the collection and the warning about fill values, and say nothing about what a reach width is. Four drafted, unsigned pages in the queue saved the survey of the file, the reach_q census and the names of the two passes whose height departs; each was checked on this file before use. Looked at the shape of the record (LOOK-001), then at every pass by name (LOOK-003, after LOOK-002 failed on a wrong assumption about the gauge block). The pass table had the width alternating between passes a day apart, so the passes were grouped into overpass tracks (LOOK-004). The grouping first used a repeat period supplied from memory; a first check of that from the file was badly scored and is a dead end (LOOK-008, NOTE-004); a grouping from the gaps alone agrees (LOOK-010). Then the relation in levels and changes, overall and by track, with and without three named passes (LOOK-005); the use as a check on height with no gauge (LOOK-006); what goes with a width off its line (LOOK-007); and pairing tolerance and the two halves of the year (LOOK-009). A line for all tracks together was tried first and is kept in the numbers above as the case where the idea does not hold.

**How many things were tried.** 10 looks were run and logged, 1 of them failed, and 0 more were refused at the fence. The lead rests on LOOK-001, LOOK-003, LOOK-004, LOOK-005, LOOK-006, LOOK-007, LOOK-009, LOOK-010. The count is taken from the exploration log, not from memory.

## Choices that would change it

- **CHOICE-002: Group the passes into three overpass tracks (A, B, C) and read the width against stage inside each track, as well as over all passes.** Alternatives: one line for all passes, tracks ignored; one constant per track removed, then pooled. Rests on: made after seeing in LOOK-003 that width alternates between passes a day apart; LOOK-004 has track A wider than track B in 17 of 17 close pairs. The grouping first used a repeat period I supplied from memory (no page states it); LOOK-010 regroups from the gaps alone and agrees on every pass once the missing 2024-03-25 pass is allowed for. LOOK-008, a first attempt at that check, was badly scored and is not relied on.. How it would change the answer: With one line for all passes and tracks ignored the correlation is +0.683 and the residual 25.1 m of width, 3.23 m in stage terms; inside tracks it is +0.762, +0.865 and +0.973 (LOOK-005). With one constant per track removed and pooled it is +0.805. The track grouping is inferred, not read from the file, which has no pass number.
- **CHOICE-003: Score levels of width against levels of stage as the main relation, and report changes between consecutive passes beside it.** Alternatives: changes between consecutive paired passes only, the signed recipe's form for height. Rests on: width is a length across the river and has no vertical reference, so the reason the signed recipe scores changes (an uncited datum offset) does not apply; and consecutive passes alternate tracks, so a change across tracks carries the track offset. Run both ways in LOOK-005: consecutive changes over all pairs correlate at +0.274, levels at +0.683.. How it would change the answer: Scored as changes between consecutive paired passes, the signed recipe's form, width against stage correlates at +0.274 over all pairs and at +0.675, +0.670 and +0.942 inside tracks A, B and C (LOOK-005). Read that way alone the answer would be that there is hardly a relation.
- **CHOICE-004: A straight line of width on stage, with the rank correlation beside every Pearson correlation.** Alternatives: a curve (a bank that flattens or a step where water reaches the batture); rank correlation alone. Rests on: the simplest form; a curve was not tried. Rank and Pearson correlations are printed together in LOOK-005.. How it would change the answer: A curve was not tried. If width steps up where water reaches ground between the levees, a straight line is the wrong form and the slope means little. The rank correlations (+0.895, +0.907, +0.936 by track) do not depend on the form.
- **CHOICE-005: Show scores with and without the pass of 2024-02-12 (width 937.3 m), singled out after looking at the pass table.** Alternatives: keep every pass in every score; a rule written before looking; none exists. Rests on: LOOK-003 and LOOK-007: its width sits 89.0 m above its track's line with nothing in the file marking it (dark_frac 0.015, reach_q 1). Leaving it out by date is a way to show what it carries, not a screen. Track A goes from r +0.762 to +0.908 without it (LOOK-005).. How it would change the answer: With the pass of 2024-02-12 left out by date, track A goes from r=+0.762 and a residual of 27.0 m to r=+0.908 and 10.5 m; all pairs go from +0.683 to +0.745 (LOOK-005). It was singled out after looking and no rule written beforehand would have named it.
- **CHOICE-007: Read 'a check on the height' as: inside each track, fit height on width leaving the pass out, and call a pass singled out when its height is more than 3 scatters from what its width implies.** Alternatives: one line for all tracks; 2 scatters; width against its own track median with no height. Rests on: chosen after seeing LOOK-005; the threshold of 3 was picked after seeing the output of LOOK-006, where it separates three passes from the rest. With one line for all tracks (also in LOOK-006) the pass of 2024-10-30 falls to rank 5 at 1.89 scatters. At 2 scatters a fourth pass, 2024-05-27, whose height agrees with the gauge, is singled out.. How it would change the answer: With one line for all tracks the pass of 2024-10-30 falls to rank 5 of 52 at -1.89 scatters and would not be singled out. At 2 scatters with track lines a fourth pass is singled out. The threshold of 3 was chosen after seeing the output (LOOK-006).

## Choices that would not

- **CHOICE-001: Pair each pass with the nearest gauge reading inside 15 minutes, as the signed recipe does; 50 of 52 pair.** Alternatives: 60 minutes, which pairs all 52; 7 minutes, 48 pairs. Why not: Pairing at 60 minutes (52 pairs) or 7 minutes (48 pairs) gives all-pairs correlations of +0.685 and +0.708 against +0.683 at 15 minutes, and track lines that move by at most a few tenths of a metre of width per metre of stage (LOOK-009).
- **CHOICE-006: Show scores with and without the two passes whose height departs from the gauge (2024-10-30, 2024-03-26), taken by date from a drafted, unsigned page in the queue and not rediscovered.** Alternatives: keep every pass; screen on reach_q or dark_frac. Why not: Without the two passes whose height departs, all pairs go from r=+0.683 to +0.706; track C is +0.973 either way and track B goes from +0.865 to +0.914 (LOOK-005). The reading, a relation inside tracks and a poor one across them, is the same.

## Pitfalls met

- Width depends on the overpass track at like stage; nothing in the knowledge names this. Raised as NOTE-001.
- One pass (2024-02-12) has a width far off its line with nothing in the file marking it, and the file has no width_u. Raised as NOTE-002.
- The file has no pass number, so tracks are inferred; the drafted page knowledge-queue/gotchas/swot-gauge-baton-rouge-fixture-lacks-processing-fields.md (a draft, not signed) names the missing fields. The repeat period is in no page: NOTE-003.
- Two passes carry most of the height disagreement; named by the drafted page knowledge-queue/gotchas/swot-gauge-change-scores-carried-by-two-passes.md (a draft, not signed) and met here in LOOK-006.
- reach_q values are undefined in the knowledge and never 0 here; drafted page knowledge-queue/gotchas/swot-reach-q-values-undefined-and-never-zero.md (a draft, not signed). No screen was written on it.
- Fill values must be filtered, not averaged: signed page connectors/hydrocron-swot.md. None met in width_m (LOOK-003).
- The gauge is provisional throughout 2024 and may be revised: signed pages recipes/swot-gauge-confrontation.md and gotchas/nwis-provisional-data.md.
- A reach width is an aggregate over roughly 10 km set beside a stage at one point: signed page gotchas/swot-reach-node-scope.md.
- The track lines were fitted and the three odd passes named on the same year. In the second half of 2024 stage only reached 6.42 m, so the half year check in LOOK-009 does not test the lines at high water.

## Proposed plan for a confirming study (a stub, to be written properly at the plan stage)

- **Question:** On passes of reach 74210000331 from 2025-01-01 onward, in SWOT_L2_HR_RiverSP_reach_D with the overpass track read from the product's pass number, does the reach width correlate with the stage at USGS-07374000 once one constant per track is removed?
- **Quantity estimated:** the Pearson correlation of width_m with gauge stage over passes paired within 15 minutes, after subtracting each track's own mean width and mean stage, with the signed recipe's sampling interval (lag-1 model, effective sample capped at the count)
- **The difference that matters:** Nobody has stated one; the request asks only whether there is a relation at all. Proposed here, for the scientist to decide: a correlation of 0.7 or more counts as a relation worth using, and one below 0.4 as none. The 2024 value this is proposed from, +0.805, was seen before the rule was proposed.
- **Rule, sketched:** Supported if the correlation is 0.7 or more and the lower end of its interval is above 0.4. Refuted if the upper end of its interval is below 0.7. Anything else is inconclusive. No pass is left out unless a screen is written before the data is opened, from the product description and not from this lead.
- **Hard cases:** A synthetic series with a track offset of 40 m and no stage relation must not pass. One with a within-track slope of 7 m per m and a single width outlier of 90 m must be scored with the outlier in. A window that crosses the change of processing identifier on 2025-04-28 (named by a drafted, unsigned page from the signed lakes page) must be scored per identifier. Fewer than ten pairs on a track must refuse. Large negative fills in width_m must be dropped by name.
- **What this could not show:** Why the tracks differ, whether the offsets hold at other reaches, and whether width can catch a wrong height: the 2024 scatter of height about width is far larger than the height errors seen, so a relation that holds would still leave the check coarse. One leveed reach says nothing about reaches that do widen freely.

Held-out data for that study: only what this exploration set aside (a later period: 2025-01-01 to onward (the window stays open)). The explored data is working data there.
