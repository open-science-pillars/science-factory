# Lead from EXPLORATION-0101 (exploring; version 1)

**Exploring.** This is a lead, not a finding. It was found by looking at data, every choice in it was made after looking, and it has been tested on nothing. It cannot be signed. To become a finding it goes through a confirming study on the data that was set aside.

**The question.** How well does SWOT RiverSP Version D reach water surface elevation follow the USGS gauge at Baton Rouge, and where does it not?

**What looks true.** On calendar 2024 at Baton Rouge (reach 74210000331, collection SWOT_L2_HR_RiverSP_reach_D, against USGS-07374000, paired within 15 minutes), the reach height looks as if it rises and falls with the gauge to within about two per cent once passes the product itself marks are left out: with reach_q below 2 and wse_u below 0.2 m the gain is 1.0140 [1.0043, 1.0236] on 46 pairs, the residual scatter is 0.0815 m against a median stated uncertainty of 0.0957 m, and the recipe's change scores become RMSD 0.1051 m and correlation +0.9962. It does not hold for every pass: with all 50 pairs the gain is 1.0345 [1.0034, 1.0656], which is not inside 0.95 to 1.05, and two passes carry 0.912 of the summed squared departure. One (2024-10-30, the year's lowest stage, reach_q 2, wse_u 0.095 m) is 1.776 m low; the other (2024-03-26, high water, reach_q 1, wse_u 0.296 m, dark_frac 0.692) is 0.947 m high and the flag does not catch it. The gain also looks slightly above one, and the lowest third of stage sits lower against the gauge than the rest (mean departure -0.0594 m against +0.0402 m and +0.0287 m); this year cannot say whether that is the instrument, the gauge's place in the reach, or a slow drift. The level difference is not scored: the two are on different vertical references.

**How it was found.** Data after calendar 2024 was set aside first, before the knowledge or the data was read. Thirteen knowledge pages were read (twelve listed by the tool and one more), all signed except one draft. The signed recipe's shipped executor was run unmodified first and its 2024 scores were reproduced exactly (LOOK-002, LOOK-003). Every pass was then listed by name against the median difference (LOOK-004), which is where the two departing passes and their product fields were seen. The request's "within about five per cent" was read as a gain, and the gain was computed under eight ways of leaving passes out and by Theil-Sen (LOOK-005), leaving each pass out in turn (LOOK-006), by stage third and half, by rising and falling water and against time (LOOK-007), with a reach-slope term (LOOK-008), under five pairing rules (LOOK-009), against the stated uncertainty and the flag (LOOK-010), by overpass track and with a time term (LOOK-011), and the two departing passes were set beside their neighbours and the gauge readings around them (LOOK-012). Tried and dropped: a split of passes by the minute of the pass time (part of LOOK-010), which was a bad key; it was redone by repeat-cycle phase. The Version C block that the working file also carries was not used.

**How many things were tried.** 12 looks were run and logged, 0 of them failed, and 0 more were refused at the fence. The lead rests on LOOK-001, LOOK-003, LOOK-004, LOOK-005, LOOK-006, LOOK-007, LOOK-008, LOOK-009, LOOK-010, LOOK-011, LOOK-012. The count is taken from the exploration log, not from memory.

## Choices that would change it

- **CHOICE-003: Read 'rise and fall with the gauge to within about five percent' as: the gain (slope of satellite elevation on gauge elevation, intercept free to absorb the datum offset) has its interval inside 0.95 to 1.05. Report the recipe's change scores beside it.** Alternatives: the recipe's change RMSD over the gauge change spread, below 0.05; the correlation of the changes above some value; the ratio of the standard deviations of the changes. Rests on: The request's wording, which is about size of rise and fall and gives no formula; the signed recipe scores changes and has no gain. Chosen after LOOK-003 and LOOK-004, knowing the change RMSD over spread is 0.3197 with all passes, so that reading would have failed outright. Not tried as a rule: the other readings.. How it would change the answer: The five per cent was read as a gain. Read as the recipe's change RMSD over the gauge change spread it is 0.3197 with all passes, far from five per cent, and it would stay above five per cent under any screen tried. The other readings (correlation, ratio of standard deviations) were not tried as rules.
- **CHOICE-004: For the headline numbers, leave out passes with reach_q at or above 2 and passes whose stated uncertainty wse_u is at or above 0.2 m. Both are read from the product before pairing and neither uses the gauge.** Alternatives: no screen, every paired pass, as the signed recipe's worked example does; reach_q below 2 only; leave out the two departing passes by date; dark_frac below 0.5 in place of the wse_u limit (the same passes on this year); a method that needs no screen (Theil-Sen). Rests on: LOOK-004: two passes carry 0.912 of the summed squared departure; one is flagged with an ordinary wse_u, one is unflagged with the year's largest wse_u (0.296 m, the rest are 0.093 to 0.110 m). The 0.2 m limit was set after seeing that one pass and is tuned on it. LOOK-005 ran every alternative.. How it would change the answer: This is the choice that moves the answer most, and the wse_u limit was set after seeing the one pass it removes. No screen: gain 1.0345 [1.0034, 1.0656], not inside 0.95 to 1.05, change RMSD 0.3823 m. reach_q below 2 only: 1.0190 [0.9968, 1.0412], inside, change RMSD 0.1859 m. With wse_u below 0.2 m as well: 1.0140 [1.0043, 1.0236]. dark_frac below 0.5 in place of the wse_u limit removes the same pass. Leaving the two passes out by date: 1.0146 [1.0053, 1.0238]. Theil-Sen on all 50 pairs with no screen: 1.0193 (no interval computed).
- **CHOICE-005: Fit the gain as a straight line with no time term and no reach-slope term for the headline.** Alternatives: add a time term, as the prototype mvp/gain.py does; add the product's reach slope as a regressor (gauge not at the reach centre); fit lower and upper halves of stage apart. Rests on: Simplest quantity that answers the request. LOOK-011 ran the time term (screened gain 1.0094, plain standard error 0.0051, against 1.0132 without) and LOOK-008 the slope term (1.0048, plain standard error 0.0085, against 1.0140 without); LOOK-007 the halves (1.0133 lower, 0.9871 upper).. How it would change the answer: Does not move whether the gain is within five per cent; does move whether it differs from one. With a time term the screened gain is 1.0094 (plain standard error 0.0051) against 1.0132 without, on all 52 passes interpolated; with the product's reach slope as a regressor it is 1.0048 (plain standard error 0.0085) against 1.0140. Slope and stage are correlated at +0.876 on the screened passes, so the two cannot be told apart here. Lower and upper halves of stage give 1.0133 and 0.9871.
- **CHOICE-006: Treat the two departing passes (2024-03-26 and 2024-10-30) as satellite departures, not gauge faults.** Alternatives: treat them as gauge errors or real differences between a reach average and a point. Rests on: LOOK-012: the gauge readings around each move by at most 0.024 m between readings, and the passes on either side sit at their usual difference from the gauge; on 2024-10-30 the satellite alone drops by about 2 m and reports a negative reach slope, on 2024-03-26 the satellite reports a slope of 135.8 ppm against 21 to 31 ppm on the neighbours. The gauge is provisional throughout, so this is a reading, not a settled fact.. How it would change the answer: Not tried as an alternative analysis. If either departing pass were a gauge fault or a real reach against point difference, leaving it out would be hiding a failure of the comparison, not of the product. The gauge is provisional throughout 2024.

## Pitfalls met

- Two vertical references: the level difference is not a bias and was not scored. recipes/swot-gauge-confrontation.md (signed by human:PaulMRamirez); gotchas/swot-gauge-datum-mismatch.md says the same for lakes and is NOT signed (a draft).
- Bad passes are not random and the flag is not enough: of three passes with reach_q 2 one departs and two do not, and the other departing pass has reach_q 1. No knowledge page says what reach_q means for a river reach or what to screen. NOTE-002.
- The signed recipe's worked example scores every pass; its change RMSD of 0.3823 m is carried mostly by two passes. NOTE-002, for the steward.
- The working file has no processing identifier, cycle, pass number or crossover quality, so the processing of 2024 is taken from datasets/swot-lakes.md (signed), not read from the data. NOTE-001.
- Version C and Version D differ by metres at this reach and the service picks one when not told. gotchas/hydrocron-collection-default.md (signed). Only Version D was used; the collection is named in the file.
- The gauge is provisional throughout 2024 and may be revised. gotchas/nwis-provisional-data.md (signed). connectors/usgs-water.md (signed) is past its stale_after date: NOTE-003.
- Residuals of the screened fit are positively autocorrelated; the interval uses a lag-1 effective sample capped at the count, as the recipe does for its own scores.
- A leak to state plainly: before any look, the example's README and the docstring of mvp/confront.py had already told this assistant that an earlier run saw a flagged low-stage pass and an unflagged high pass on this year. The passes were found again by name in LOOK-004, but the looking was not blind to them.

## Proposed plan for a confirming study (a stub, to be written properly at the plan stage)

- **Question:** On the passes over reach 74210000331 after calendar 2024, with passes of reach_q at or above 2 or wse_u at or above 0.2 m left out before pairing, does the gain of SWOT Version D reach height on the Baton Rouge gauge stay within five per cent of one?
- **Quantity estimated:** The gain: the slope of satellite reach elevation on gauge elevation across passes paired within 15 minutes by the shipped executor, with a free intercept for each processing identifier so that a step between reprocessed and forward-processed passes cannot leak into it, and an interval from the lag-1 effective sample of the residuals capped at the count.
- **The difference that matters:** The scientist's own number, five per cent, given in the request as a judgment and not a requirement: the gain between 0.95 and 1.05. Proposed here as the rule's bounds, for the scientist to decide.
- **Rule, sketched:** Supports the idea: the 95 per cent interval of the gain lies inside 0.95 to 1.05. Refutes it: the interval lies wholly outside that range. Anything else, including fewer than ten pairs in a processing group, passes that carry no processing identifier, or the executor declining, is inconclusive. Reported beside it and used by no rule: the same gain with no screen, with reach_q alone, with a time term, by half of stage, and the recipe's change scores.
- **Hard cases:** From the pitfalls: a flagged gross error at the lowest stage; an unflagged gross error at high water with a large stated uncertainty; an unflagged gross error with an ordinary stated uncertainty (which this screen would not catch); an offset step between processing identifiers; a slow drift; a relation that bends with stage; residual autocorrelation; gauge readings only hourly around some passes; a datum offset of any size, which must not move the gain.
- **What this could not show:** One reach and one gauge on a leveed lower river: nothing about ungauged reaches, which is the scientist's real purpose. A gain inside five per cent would not say the level can be used, since the datum offset stays uncited. It would not say why the gain sits above one. The screen was tuned on one pass, so a pass in the later data may fail in a third way. If the gauge stays provisional, a later revision could move the answer.

Held-out data for that study: only what this exploration set aside (a later period: 2025-01-01 to onward (the window stays open)). The explored data is working data there.
